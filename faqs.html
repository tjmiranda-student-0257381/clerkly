Getting started

A virtual assistant takes on the recurring operational work of running a business, remotely. In practice that means inboxes, calendars, data entry, research, customer replies, order processing, invoicing admin — the work that must happen but doesn’t need the founder to do it.

Clerkly goes further than most: alongside the admin I build and maintain websites, wire up automations and handle technical tasks that a typical assistant would need to outsource.

A simple test: for one week, note every task you do that someone else could do with clear instructions. If that list runs to more than five hours, you have a case. If it’s mostly decisions and relationships, you probably don’t need an assistant yet — and I’ll tell you so on the call.

Typically within a week of agreeing scope, and sometimes within 48 hours if a slot is open. Onboarding itself takes about two days: access sharing, a priority list, and the first batch of tasks queued up.

Yes. Most cautious clients start with a five-hour hourly block on real work — not a made-up test task. If it goes well we move to a retainer; if it doesn’t, you’ve spent a small amount and still got the work done.

Pricing & billing

  • Hourly: $35/hour, five-hour minimum, for irregular work.
  • Starter retainer: $600/month for 20 hours ($30/hr effective).
  • Growth retainer: $1,400/month for 50 hours ($28/hr effective).
  • Scale retainer: $2,600/month for 100 hours ($26/hr effective).
  • Fixed-price projects: from $950, quoted per scope.

Full detail is on the services page. Prices are in U.S. dollars and exclude any taxes that may apply in your jurisdiction.

Retainers are invoiced in advance on the first working day of each month. Hourly work is invoiced at month end. Projects are 50% up front and 50% on delivery. Invoices are payable within 7 days by bank transfer, card or PayPal.

Unused hours roll over once, into the following month only. They aren’t refundable and don’t accumulate indefinitely — the retainer reserves capacity in my schedule, which has a real cost whether or not it’s used.

Security & privacy

Through a password manager — 1Password, Bitwarden or similar — never over email or chat. Where a platform supports delegated access (Google Workspace, Shopify staff accounts, Meta Business Manager), I use that instead of shared credentials.

  • Two-factor authentication enabled everywhere it’s offered
  • Least-privilege permissions — only what a task requires
  • A work device with disk encryption and an up-to-date OS
  • All access revoked and confirmed in writing at offboarding

Yes to both. Send your own NDA or use mine. If you process personal data under GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA or similar rules, I’ll sign a data processing agreement setting out how I handle it as your processor.

Within 30 days of an engagement ending I return or hand over your files, remove my access from your systems, and delete local copies of your data except where I’m legally required to keep records (invoices, for instance). You get written confirmation when it’s done. See the Privacy Policy for the detail.

I won’t take on a direct competitor in the same niche and market while we’re working together, and I’ll tell you if a potential conflict comes up. Client information is never shared between engagements, full stop.
